# Credits: the Russian translation

## Translation memory

- **Skadga/nethack-ru** — <https://github.com/Skadga/nethack-ru>, a Russian
  translation of NetHack 5.0 for Windows, NetHack General Public License.
  The first draft of the names in `glossary.ru.toml` was taken from its
  `monsters.h`, `objects.h`, `artilist.h`, `role.c`, `defsym.h` and the
  other name tables (its texts, not its code). Every entry taken from it is
  marked `src = "nethack-ru"`; the ones changed here say what it wrote.

## Russian forms

- **pymorphy3** — <https://github.com/no-plagiarism/pymorphy3>, MIT, with
  its Russian dictionary (pymorphy3-dicts-ru, built from the
  **OpenCorpora** corpus, <https://opencorpora.org>): `tools/i18n/lexicon_build.py`
  drafts the case forms in `lexicon.ru.toml` with it. Neither ships with
  the game.

## Names in established Russian

Where a creature or a thing already has a Russian name, the glossary uses
it rather than inventing one; its note names the source. They come from the
Russian editions of *Dungeons & Dragons* (the D&D monster names: бурый
увалень, ржавник...), J. R. R. Tolkien (умертвие, призрак,
урук-хай, Оркрист, Жало), Lewis Carroll in D. Orlovskaya's translation
(бармаглот, Вострый Клинок), Terry Pratchett (Двацветок, Анк-Морпорк, Слепой Ио, Оффлер,
Госпожа), Fritz Leiber (Иссек, Мог, Кос), M. Moorcock (Буревестник) and
Russian mythology, history and natural history (левкрота, сулица, бердыш).
